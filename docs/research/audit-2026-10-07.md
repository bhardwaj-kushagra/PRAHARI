## 3. What the evidence supports

### 3.1 Strong (SIM)

1. **A rigorous evaluation method.** Three pre-registered rounds on fresh seeds compare detectors at equal network
   false-alarm rates, with git history proving the order, on an open, deterministic, provenance-tagged testbed. Results
   that went against the authors' own design are reported. This is the paper's most defensible contribution
   (direction, contribution 1).
2. **The budgeted chain against classical detectors (R1, family A).**
   - At 10 false incidents a month, P2 confirms 83.3% of fires within 3 h.
   - That is +36.8, +51.6 and +32.6 points over a fixed threshold, replay-tuned v1 and an AR(1) residual chart, with
     Holm p ≤ 1.2e-15.
   - The textbook ARL threshold gives 123.9–170.5 false incidents a month at every sweep point.
3. **Common-mode strategies and where each one breaks** (contribution 3).
   - Median subtraction does well under synchronous haze (R1: 92.1% at 10 a month) but fails under uneven haze (R2
     H-mix: floor 4.22 a month, 62.5% at B* = 10).
   - Median + SCMR detects most at 10 a month: R2 92.2%; R3 90.6% against the gate's 87.5%.
   - Mei's network sum cannot hold any pre-set budget without alarming continuously.
   - In R3 the ranking changes with the budget. The gate leads at 1 a month (H1: −8.7 points for median + SCMR, Holm
     p 0.012); median + SCMR leads at 3, 10 and 30 a month (descriptive).
4. **The false alarms that remain are smoke, for SCMR, gate and AR methods.**
   - P2's floor incidents start inside haze: 99.4% in R1; 90.9–100.0% across R2's scenarios; 83.5–97.0% across R3's.
   - The gate is at 100.0% and AR at 99.5–100.0%.
   - **Median-referenced methods carry a large outside-haze share:** P2-medSCMR 16.7–90.6% inside (R2) and 45.5–88.9%
     (R3); P2-med 56.8–88.9% (R2).

### 3.2 Moderate (REAL; contribution 4)

- **Node calibration carries over to real sensors.**
  - N5 quiet networks give 0.00–1.69 candidates per node per 30 d against a target of 1.
  - Delhi's 15-minute data in the smoke season give 1.41.
  - This is the first half of the paper's decomposition.
- **Real network-wide events are rarer and more even than M20b's draws:**
  - rates of ×0.05–×0.31 of H-mix's in NW India (pooled per cluster; single cluster-years up to ×0.33);
  - amplitude CVs mostly below the operator's interval.
- **The floor's size cannot be measured on real data:** 1–5 events per cluster; PM₂.₅ and CO at km spacing, not
  MOX at 70 m.

## 4. Problems the paper must address

### 4.1 R1's "floor of about 5 a month" came from SCMR's fixed ratio, and no page said so

- **R1:** P2 used SCMR's ratio threshold fixed at ρ = 3, and its floor was 5.07 false incidents a month.
- **R2/R3:** with ρ as a second knob (direction, fix 2), each scenario's lowest rate for P2 fell, at about the same
  detection at that setting.

| Scenario | Lowest rate at ρ = 3 (detection) | Lowest rate at any ρ (detection) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R2 H-sync (R1's haze model) | 4.52 (56.2%) | 0.22 at ρ = 10 (55.2%) |
| R2 H-mix | 5.47 (55.6%) | 0.56 at ρ = 10 (54.5%) |
| R3 H-sync | 5.94 (54.1%) | 0.40 at ρ = 10 (58.2%) |
| R3 H-mix | 5.22 (46.6%) | 0.73 at ρ = 10 (45.6%) |

`numbers.md` rows "Audit … lowest false incidents/month at ρ = 3" give every R2 and R3 scenario.

**Consequences:**

- **The working title overstates.** "The common-mode floor limits false alarms" does not hold as stated: with the
  strictest common-mode rejection, P2's floor is 0.22–1.28 a month across R2 and R3.
- **R1's revised claim C2** ("median referencing beats SCMR") rests partly on ρ = 3.
  - On synchronous haze, P2 at ρ = 10 has a lower floor than median subtraction (R2 H-sync: 0.22 against 0.88).
  - At 1 a month there, P2 detects more at equal false alarms (75.2% against 51.3%).
  - Median subtraction still detects more at 10 a month (93.2% against 89.0%).
- **What does hold:**
  - the remaining false alarms are smoke (§3.1, point 4);
  - strict budgets cost detection. P2 confirms 90.5% at 10 a month (R2) but 57.2% at 1 a month in R3 (69.8% at
    equal false alarms).
- **Is that cost caused by haze?** It is shown only indirectly.
  - R1's no-haze sweep (10 seeds, ρ = 3): P2 detects 90.1% at 1 a month and 92.3% at 3 a month.
  - With the default haze it cannot reach either budget.
  - This is one knob, ten seeds and a different haze model. No paired, pre-registered counterfactual exists under the
    final methods.

**Mechanism (DER, from the configuration):**

- SCMR's ratio is f_loc / max(f_net, 1/N) (M31).
- After siting, 64 of the 100 nodes have 9-node neighbourhoods (R = 1.6 × 70 m); the other 36 sit on the edges with 6
  or 4.
- So an interior cluster's ratio cannot exceed 100/9 ≈ 11.1, and reaches it only when no other node in the network
  has a candidate in the window.
- **For an interior cluster, ρ = 10 therefore means "confirm only while the rest of the network is quiet".** The low
  floor is bought by holding alarms whenever anything else is happening, including during regional smoke.
- R3 shows the cost at 1 a month: P2 confirms 45.3% of the fires whose first 3 h overlap haze and 57.9% of the others.

### 4.2 Grid edges: mostly natural limits, except the node target

Several selected settings sit on the strict end of their grids (rows "Audit … on a strict grid edge"):

- ρ = 10 for P2 and median + SCMR at the floor and at 1 and 3 a month;
- θ = 0.05 for the gate's floor;
- the node target r = 0.1 for several floors.

- **ρ:** the ceiling in §4.1 makes ρ = 10 effectively the strictest useful value for interior clusters. Any ρ above
  100/9 ≈ 11.1 would hold every interior cluster.
- **θ = 0.05:** this means about 5 nodes with evidence. θ = 0.02 would hold every alarm backed by two nodes (the
  smallest quorum), so much lower values would hold the fires themselves.
- **So these floors are not cut off by the grids.** But they are conditional on the network geometry and the
  clustering form: the ceiling is N divided by the neighbourhood size (legacy clustering).
- **The node target r = 0.1 is a real edge.** Whether stricter node thresholds lower the floor is exactly the
  direction's claim ("node thresholds cannot move it"), and it was not tested below r = 0.1.

### 4.3 The floor estimand can reward switching off

- A method's floor is its lowest false-alarm rate, whatever its detection.
- For the gate this picks a nearly silent setting: it confirms 1.7% of fires at its floor setting in R2 and 1.4% in
  R3.
- So "the gate has the lowest floor" (R2 family F; R3 H2) is true but says little.
- P2's floor setting, in contrast, still detects (54.5% in R2).
- **The informative comparison near strict budgets is detection at equal false alarms.** At 1 a month in R3 H-mix:
  P2 69.8%, the gate 47.0%, median + SCMR 40.6% (descriptive).

### 4.4 Strict-budget operating points are noisy with 20 selection seeds

Median + SCMR's 1-a-month setting gave 1.0 false incident a month on R3's selection seeds and 1.24 on its test seeds.

### 4.5 Realism of the haze scenarios for MOX

- **The real data suggest even haze.** Network-wide events are more even than M20b draws. That is the regime where
  median subtraction did well (R1, H-sync).
- **MOX sensitivity spread is not measured.** Low-cost optical PM sensors include their own unit-to-unit spread.
  MOX sensors are generally reported to differ more between units (a literature point for the authors to check and
  cite), and no dataset used here measures that spread for a MOX network.
- **So it is open which scenario is realistic for a MOX network.**

### 4.6 The novelty claim needs checking

The claim is that no ground-sensor wildfire study reports detection at a fixed network false-alarm rate. It rests on
the studies reviewed so far, and the authors should verify it against the literature before stating it.

## 5. Verdict

- **Is the evidence a worthy advance?** For a regional IEEE conference paper (sensors/IoT track), yes. The method is
  rigorous and transparent, the comparative results are clear, the failure modes are documented, and real-data
  checks back the premises. This is more than most of the field reports.
- **Is it ready under the current title and claims?** No.
  - §4.1 changes the central claim from "an unmovable floor" to "regional smoke sets the price of strict false-alarm
    budgets".
  - That price has only indirect support under the final methods.
- **For a journal:** a paired no-haze counterfactual, a stricter node-target range, and ideally a real MOX background
  check (plan B6) would answer the obvious reviews.
- **Is everything done?** No.
  - Every experiment in the direction was run.
  - Still open:
    - the reframing, and one confirmatory round to support it;
    - the paper figures and tables;
    - the release tag and DOI;
    - the authors' writing.
